Terrain, integer levels, final walk mask and cliff/ramp diagnostics share the same grid coordinates; all world positions remain at Y=0. The drawing is schematic, not final AoE-style texture assets.

The generator samples noise temporarily, quantizes it to integer tiers, performs three local majority passes to remove threshold speckles and flattens each base core with a two-cell safety collar. Returned height data is an integer tier array. Neighboring unequal tiers generate a blocked band on the lower side. Thickness is absolute tier delta times cliffCells, using Manhattan expansion within the lower tier. At corners and junctions these bands overlap and may be thicker.

Ramps are rectangular cell corridors selected on region interfaces. Each longitudinal lane must have clear landings on opposite tiers and one tier transition. A corridor may reopen cliff cells but never rocks, resources or the protected base core. Wider/higher interfaces can have multiple spaced portals. Corridor validation can fail; the supplementary connectivity pass attempts every island connection, only converts unconnectable small pockets within the caps into mountains, and rejects larger failures. Returned playable noise maps must have exactly one walk island; competitive balance is not guaranteed. Set all cluster quotas to zero to isolate terrain baking.

Final physical occupancy combines ground, cliff bands and ramp openings. Radius clearance generates a conservative center-position walk mask (blocked-square distance lower bound, radius plus 0.05 cell margin). A one-cell portal can serve a sufficiently small unit; increasing radius can close it. Dijkstra and components read only this plain mask, with no corner cutting, no height metadata, no edge link permissions and no slope/Y calculation. Animated dots follow the computed grid route; ORCA and formation movement are not simulated.

## Cluster resources

Cluster bounds never block ground. `RTSClusters.bakeResourceOccupancy` stamps only individual 2×2 prefab footprints into resourceMask; final physical is terrainPhysical minus that mask. Empty cells preserve terrain occupancy, while walk clearance still accounts for unit radius around actual resources. `node Docs/Multiplayer/Labs/lab5-resource-occupancy-tests.cjs` verifies full-grid occupancy differences, unchanged mountain masks, empty ground inside cluster circles and one final walk island on seeds 30000/1337/2000.

Mountains have a persistent mask sampled before cliff baking, without road or resource exemptions. Small opposite-cell gaps are closed by adding mountain cells. Every connected mountain component receives one integer tier, so internal cliff bands cannot divide it. Cliff bands stop at mountain boundaries; ramp corridors and resource footprints cannot reopen mountain cells. Only an unconnectable pocket within the 64-cell and 2% caps becomes mountain occupancy; larger failures reject the candidate.

Run `node Docs/Multiplayer/Labs/lab5-mountain-tests.cjs` to verify seeds 1, 42 and 1337: all original mountain cells survive, adjacent mountain cells share a tier, and no mountain cell becomes a cliff, ramp, physical opening or walkable cell after resource generation.

Resources now use Lab 4's four regional roles: main and secondary annuli around a base, a farther advantage annulus and central contested sectors. Controls specify cluster quotas for each role and integer min/max prefab counts within a cluster. Count means prefab instances, not resource amount stored inside the prefab. A cluster is admitted as a whole or rejected; rejected clusters are counted in requested versus placed status.

Every 2×2 prefab footprint in a cluster must have the exact same integer HeightLevel as the cluster. Prefabs cannot overwrite cliffs, ramps or existing occupancy. Clusters are spaced apart; members are sampled within a compact radius with spacing so their gathering perimeter is not automatically sealed. The unused circle is only a visual cluster boundary; its empty cells can contain other terrain.

Each trial bakes all candidate footprints, recomputes radius clearance and connected components, then revalidates every prefab of every existing cluster. Each private cluster needs one common reachable navigation component from its owner base. Each contested cluster needs a common component reached by at least two bases. Gathering positions must be on the same tier as the resource and within ceil(radius + 1) cells of its footprint. Every trial must retain exactly one walk island and valid base sources when ramps are enabled. The same audit is repeated after all clusters are placed. Click a resource to show a route from an accessible base to a gathering position.

Ramp min/max controls vary portal widths. Per interface, the first valid portal uses the minimum, a second uses the maximum, and further portals sample the range. Large portals may not fit the interface; the bake does not force invalid ramps. The minimum and maximum can be equal for fixed-width tests.

Run `node Docs/Multiplayer/Labs/lab5-cluster-tests.cjs` to independently verify min/max counts, all footprint levels, final reachability from advertised bases, ramp bounds and 320px layout in both demo and noise scenes. The combined experiment does not establish competitive balance or guarantee all requested clusters fit every seed.
